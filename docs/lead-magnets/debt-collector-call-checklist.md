# When a debt collector calls
### A calm, one-page checklist. You have more rights than they'll mention.

A collection call can feel like an ambush. It isn't. You're allowed to slow it down, ask
questions, and decide nothing on the spot. Here's exactly how.

## First - before you agree to anything
- **Stay calm and commit to nothing.** You can always say, "I'm not discussing this today."
- **Don't confirm the debt is yours,** and **don't promise a payment** on the call. On
  older debts, even acknowledging it or paying a little can restart the clock (see below).
- **Find out who you're actually talking to** before you share anything.

## Ask these questions (and get the answers in writing)
- What is your name, and what company are you with?
- Who is the **original creditor**, and what is the exact amount?
- Will you **send me written validation of this debt?**

## 5 things a collector legally cannot do (FDCPA)
1. Call you at unusual times - generally not before **8am or after 9pm** your time.
2. Keep calling you **at work** once you tell them your employer doesn't allow it.
3. **Threaten or harass you** - no abuse, no repeated nuisance calls, no false threats.
4. **Pretend to be** a lawyer, the government, or law enforcement.
5. **Ignore a written "stop contacting me"** request once they receive it.

## Your moves this week
- [ ] **Request validation in writing within 30 days** of first contact. Until they
      validate, they're generally supposed to pause collection.
- [ ] **Keep a log:** date, time, company, and what was said on every call.
- [ ] **Pull your free credit reports** at **annualcreditreport.com** (free; doesn't hurt
      your score) so you can see every account.
- [ ] If the debt is **old**, learn your **state's statute of limitations** before you pay
      or promise anything - the window to be sued may have passed.

## Red flags it may be a scam
- Refuses to identify the company or the original creditor.
- Demands payment by **gift card, wire, or crypto.**
- Threatens **immediate arrest** or says you must pay "right now."
- Pressures you to act before you can verify anything.

## Where to verify or report
- **CFPB** - consumerfinance.gov (submit a complaint, read your rights)
- **FTC** - reportfraud.ftc.gov
