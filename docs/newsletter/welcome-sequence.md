## Email 2 - Day 2

**Subject:** 5 things a debt collector legally cannot do
**Subject A/B:** Know this before a collector calls again
**Preheader:** The FDCPA gives you more protection than most people realize.

Hi {{first_name|there}},

If a debt has gone to collections, the single most powerful thing you can have is not
money - it's knowing the rules. A federal law, the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act
(FDCPA), puts hard limits on what collectors can do. Most people never get told this.

Five of them, in plain English:

1. **They can't call you at all hours.** Generally not before 8am or after 9pm your time.
2. **They can't call you at work** once you tell them your employer doesn't allow it.
3. **They have to prove the debt.** Ask in writing within 30 days of first contact and
   they must send validation - what you owe and to whom - before continuing to collect.
4. **They can't threaten or harass you.** No abuse, no false threats of arrest, no
   pretending to be a lawyer or the government.
5. **They have to honor "stop contacting me" in writing.** After that, they can only
   reach out for specific, limited reasons.

None of this makes a debt disappear. But it changes who's in control of the
conversation - and it's the foundation everything in Detta is built on.

If a collector has crossed one of these lines, the CFPB takes complaints at
consumerfinance.gov, and you may have rights worth talking to a licensed attorney about.

We'll send you one of these - a single, real consumer right, explained in under a
minute - in every weekly note. We call it **"One right you have."**

## Email 3 - Day 4

**Subject:** Do you actually need a debt settlement company?
**Subject A/B:** The third option nobody tells you about
**Preheader:** Most ads give you two choices. There's a quieter one.

Hi {{first_name|there}},

When you're behind, the internet gives you two loud options: keep struggling on your
own, or hand everything to a debt-settlement company. There's a quieter third option in
the middle, and it's the one the regulators actually point people to.

A quick, honest map of the real choices:

- **Debt management plan (DMP)** - a nonprofit credit counselor consolidates your
  payments. Useful for some; it's a structured program, not a quick fix.
- **Debt settlement company** - they negotiate to pay less than you owe, usually take
  control of your money, and charge **15-25% of your enrolled debt** in fees. It can
  work, but it's expensive and it's the most heavily regulated for a reason.
- **Bankruptcy** - a real legal tool for the right situation; talk to an attorney.
- **Doing it yourself** - calling your creditor, asking for a hardship plan, validating
  a debt, settling directly. The FTC's own guidance says you can do this on your own,
  for free.

That last path is the one most people are never shown how to walk - because nobody makes
15-25% when you do it yourself. That's the gap Detta exists to fill: not to do it for
you, but to hand you the plan, the scripts, and the templates so doing it yourself
feels less like a leap.

No path is right for everyone, and we'd rather you choose with clear eyes than sign up
for anything - including us - in a panic.
